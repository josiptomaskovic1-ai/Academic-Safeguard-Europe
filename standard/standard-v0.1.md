## Purpose

The StudentSafe Standard (formerly the ASE Safeguarding Standard) is an independent draft published for consultation by StudentSafe Europe under its Academic Safeguard Europe research programme. It is not an instrument of the European Union, the European Higher Education Area or any government or quality-assurance agency. It describes the published institutional safeguards that allow students to raise concerns about the conduct of people who hold academic authority over them, without risking their education.

Academic authority is the power to assess, supervise, admit, progress, examine, recommend or credit a student. It is necessary and, in the great majority of cases, exercised responsibly. The Standard exists because, when it is misused, the student often depends on the person or unit they would need to complain about.

The Standard does not assume that academic staff are dangerous. Good safeguards also protect staff by providing fair, predictable procedures.

## What the Standard covers

- **Misuse of academic authority:** using assessment, supervision, progression, references or research credit for improper purposes.
- **Retaliation:** detriment to a student because they raised a concern, complained or acted as a witness.
- **Intimidation and harassment:** in the context of academic authority.
- **Conflicts of interest:** in assessment, supervision, complaint handling and appeals.
- **Procedural unfairness:** in how concerns are received, investigated and decided.
- **Repair of academic harm:** after a concern is upheld.

## What the Standard does not cover

- **Individuals.** The Standard is applied to national frameworks and institutions, never to named people.
- **Allegations.** Whether a specific allegation is true is outside its scope.
- **Implementation in practice.** The Standard assesses documented systems. A strong formal policy does not necessarily demonstrate effective implementation, and absence of publicly identified evidence does not necessarily demonstrate that no safeguard exists.
- **Research misconduct.** Research misconduct such as plagiarism and fabrication falls outside the Standard, except where research-credit decisions are used against students.
- **General student welfare and staff-only employment procedures.**

## The twelve safeguards

1. **Definition of abuse of academic authority.** Rules must name misuse of authority over students as misconduct. Otherwise concerns fall between general ethics duties and nothing.
2. **Explicit anti-retaliation protection.** Students must be protected when they report, and the protection must include academic retaliation through grades, supervision and references.
3. **Independent complaint mechanism.** There must be a route to a body outside the line management of the person concerned.
4. **Conflict-of-interest and recusal rules.** Nobody should decide a matter in which they are involved, including an assessment of a student who has complained about them.
5. **Interim protective measures.** Practical steps, such as an alternative examiner or supervisor, should protect the student's studies while a concern is handled, without presuming guilt.
6. **Procedural deadlines.** Binding timeframes prevent concerns from being outlasted.
7. **Written, reasoned decisions.** The complainant must be able to understand the outcome and challenge it.
8. **Appeal or independent review.** A different body should be able to review the outcome.
9. **Proportionate consequences.** Consequences must be graduated and defined, including the power to limit authority over affected students.
10. **Remediation of academic harm.** Tainted assessments must be repairable through re-assessment by a different examiner, annulment, extension or a change of supervisor.
11. **Professional-boundary and safeguarding training.** Training must cover the fair use of authority, professional boundaries and anti-retaliation duties.
12. **Academic Authority Safeguarding Review.** Professional conduct relevant to supervision and assessment should be considered when conferring substantial authority over students, and periodically afterwards.

### A note on safeguard 12

An Academic Authority Safeguarding Review concerns **professional conduct relevant to positions of authority**:

- supervisory boundaries;
- conflict management;
- assessment responsibilities;
- anti-retaliation obligations;
- the responsible exercise of authority.

It is **not** a psychiatric or medical evaluation. It never concerns:

- mental or physical health;
- political views, ideology or protected beliefs;
- private personality traits unrelated to professional duties.

## Levels

Each safeguard is assessed at one of four levels (0–3), or recorded as *unable to assess* (`?`) or *not researched* (`NR`). The observable criteria for every level are defined in the scoring rubric and shown below. The rubric file `methodology/scoring-rubric.yml` is authoritative.

## Levels of application

- **National level:** what legislation and national regulators provide directly or require of higher-education institutions.
- **Institution level:** what an institution's own published rules provide, including national rules that those rules expressly apply.

National and institutional results answer different questions and are never merged.

## Versioning and consultation

Version 0.1.1 is a draft for consultation. Proposals for changes are submitted through the methodology proposal process and recorded in the public changelog. Every finding records the methodology version it was assessed under, so results under different versions are never silently mixed.
